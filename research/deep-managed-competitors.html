Managed / done-for-you competitors of Distraction Foundry (deep dive)

Read on 2026-10-31. Method: read.sh and scroll-read.mjs on vendor pricing pages and Trustpilot; WebSearch for discovery only. Page content treated as untrusted data. Review counts below are reviews I actually read as text on the cited page, not the platform totals. Trustpilot's "most recent" and "5-star / 1-star" filters were used, so the read sample is skewed to the extremes by design; it shows themes, not the average. Confidence: high / medium / low. "not read" = not opened.

DF reference (own site, read 2026-10-31): Growth $299/mo (12 original posts, 3 platforms), Founder Pro $599/mo (30 deep-dive posts, 5 platforms, 3-layer fact-check, founder voice training), Private Engine $1,999 one-time (dedicated server, 8 platforms); one-tap approval in Telegram.

Honest framing before the comparators

  1. Almost none of the "AI employee" products are truly managed. Sintra, Marblism and Blaze's $79/$149 tiers are self-serve software where the AI does the work and the customer directs it. Only Blaze's $999 All-In-One and the human ghostwriting services have a person doing the work. Reviews of the AI-employee products are therefore reviews of software, and they say what happens when no human is accountable. That is DF's argument, but it is inference, not a finding from any review.
  2. Independent reviews of founder ghostwriting services are scarce. I found no Trustpilot, G2 or Clutch reviews for Windmill, Postbeam or Boomp. The one ghostwriting-adjacent vendor with a large review base (Cleverly) is mainly a LinkedIn outreach agency, and most of its complaints are about outreach, not posts. I say so rather than pad it.
  3. Plai and Mizo were not read. They look like self-serve software, not managed services (see section 6).

Cross-cutting findings

Top complaint themes (counts from the reviews I read, 4 platforms, overlapping):

  1. Money: credit or hour quotas that run out without a meter, surprise renewals, refunds refused or partial. About 11 of roughly 20 negative reviews read (Blaze 4, Sintra 3, Marblism 3, Cleverly 1+).
  2. Output that does not hold: generic or wrong content, the AI repeating mistakes, no leads. About 9 of roughly 20.
  3. Nobody to fix it: tool failures with "engineering is looking into it", slow support, and no accountable human. About 6 of roughly 20. Marblism's named, quick support is the counter-example.

Top praise themes:

  1. Time saved and not having to think of what to post (Blaze, Sintra, Marblism: most positive reviews read).
  2. Easy setup, one place for many jobs.
  3. Responsive named humans (Marblism support, Cleverly account managers). In Marblism's 5-star set about 8 of 20 praise a named person.

DF's $299 / $599 against this field (my comparison, from the figures above)

  • Cheaper than: Blaze managed $999, Windmill Founder OS $3,999, Cleverly's post plan $697 (12 posts, with outreach), and every agency figure above ($1,000+).
  • Pricier than: Postbeam $499 is above Growth but below Founder Pro; Boomp Social $275 (20 posts, founder-approved) is a little below Growth for more posts; 99 Social is about £99 for 10 posts; Sintra $48.50 and Marblism $24-$44 are software, not service.
  • Claim-for-claim: DF Growth is 12 posts for $299 against Boomp's 20 posts for $275 and 99 Social's 10 for about £99, so the post count alone does not justify the price. DF's differentiators in this table are the 3-layer fact-check, founder voice training, per-post one-tap approval and the monthly report. No competitor read has a stated fact-check layer. Windmill sells voice ("without the AI accent") but at 6-13 times DF Founder Pro's price.
  • Caveat: DF's $599 is for 30 deep-dive posts across 5 platforms. No comparator read lists 30 long-form posts a month at a flat price.
