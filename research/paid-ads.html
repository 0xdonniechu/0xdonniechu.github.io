2. Answers

(1) Where is the gap in the buyer's pain

Evidence: Segwise's own category write-up lists the open problems.

  • Generation tools make many ads but cannot say why one won. Confidence: medium (vendor source, but consistent with the product table: most winners above generate).
  • Prediction needs account history, so new accounts and new brands get nothing. Scores do not replace testing. Confidence: medium.
  • Money is flowing to "generate then publish then read back" loops (Runway Ads, Melius, Creatify, Lapis, InstaAgent). That is crowded and funded; a solo founder will not out-produce it. Confidence: high.
  • Nobody found sells a plain, independent verdict before the spend: "of these 20 candidates, run these 3, kill these 17, here is why, for this audience and this platform". Prediction tools exist (Pencil, Extuitive, AdCreative.ai) but their scores are opaque and tied to a generator. Confidence: low-medium; absence of evidence from a US-only search, not proof of absence.
  • Chat-with-your-ad-data alone earns nothing (Ads Gateway, $10 all time). Confidence: high.
  • Brand and celebrity buyers: no product found aimed at them specifically; the tools target performance/DTC marketers. Confidence: low.

(2) Which gap fits someone who can judge what will perform or go viral but does not make creative by hand

The "selector/director" seat: pre-spend selection and creative direction. Product shape: a person-plus-agent service that takes a pile of candidate ads or concepts (from any generator, any agency, the brand's own library) and returns a ranked, reasoned go / kill list per platform (Meta, TikTok, Google), with the hook, format and audience reasoning, and then a post-test read of why winners won.

  • It passes the filter in principles.md: scores and selects, does not make media, the owner can tell whether the pick is peak, the model drafts the analysis and the owner's judgment decides.
  • It sits above the crowded generators: it is the layer that decides which of their output is worth spending on. Confidence: medium.
  • Risk: single-feature wrapper. The moat has to be the owner's track record of calls (a public scored-prediction log). Confidence: medium.
  • Adjacent fit with the YC "Reforged Labs / Boa" idea (which ads perform and why). That one answers from data; the gap is the human verdict before data exists. Confidence: low.

(3) One small paid offer, sellable this month

"Creative Verdict": the buyer sends 10 to 30 candidate ads (images, videos or scripts) plus platform, audience and budget. In 48 hours they get a one-page ranked list: run / test / kill, one reason per item, and the three hooks to make next (the brief to hand to whoever makes them). Price to test: about 150 to 300 USD per pack, one-off. For reference, the cheapest analytics tools above run 49 to 159 USD per month and the generators 59 to 300 USD per month (Ad Machine), so a one-off verdict at this price is in range; the price itself is a guess to test, not a researched number. Sell it to: DTC brands and small agencies running Meta/TikTok. Proof asset: publish 10 predictions, then the results 2 weeks later. Confidence: medium that it can be sold; low on price.
