Answers

  1. What gets an expert-judgment business to $50k-$120k MRR. From these four, only two are expert-led (Windmill, VirtualJK) and they get there by opposite routes. Windmill: about 30 clients at about $4k each, a bench doing the labour, the expert's value being the strategy and the match. VirtualJK: about 93 subscribers at about $520 average, judgment encoded once. Common features: a defined buyer with budget (B2B founders, coaches), a recurring price, a proof story with the client's money in it, and a hard cap or a software layer so the founder's hours are not the unit. Brevilabs and White Glove show the other two outcomes (labour sold at $45k with no proof; volume content with no expert). [M: n=2 relevant rows]

  2. Personal brand dependence. Not clearly. VirtualJK's founder has only 1,288 X followers and still shows 93 subscriptions, so audience size is not the only path; his sold asset is a track record ($25M of consulting sales), self-reported. Windmill sells the bench and case studies of clients, and the site names no founder. So the owner's track record can plausibly replace a following if it is stated as checkable numbers: 10 years of calling viral media, 3 agencies, a SaaS exit. The three agencies add something neither row shows: their own campaign history as proof and as first buyers. How each row's buyers actually arrive (outbound, content, referrals) is not in public data. [L]

  3. Three product shapes and first paid offers (prices are guesses, not from the research):

    • Service: "go / test / kill" verdict on a planned campaign or piece of media before the spend, delivered in 48 hours with a one-page decision. First offer: $300-$500 per verdict, sold to 5 existing clients of the 3 agencies this month. The owner judges; the agents scan and draft.
    • Replica: an AI trained on the owner's viral-judgment calls (scored examples), that scores a draft and says why. First offer: a weekly paid plan in the VirtualJK style, but only after 30-50 owner-scored items exist; before that, a paid pilot of $97-$500 on manual scoring.
    • Software + service: a scoring workspace plus a monthly review by the owner for agencies (retainer-like, Windmill's tiering: a low tier with the tool, a high tier with the owner's call). First offer: one agency, a 30-day paid pilot at about $1,000-$2,000, tool output reviewed by the owner each week. All three fit the filter in principles.md (small, sold by hand, taste is the moat); the replica is most at risk of the "model makes the judgment" failure until the owner's scoring is the training data.

  4. Past one person (the YC angle). Windmill is the only row with evidence of a team (named bench writers; labour-bound, capped intake). VirtualJK has no team on the page and one founder. So in these rows the company is one expert plus either a bench or a model; no row shows several experts. The federation model in thesis.md would be new, not copied. An honest claim: the product can grow past the founder only if judgment is encoded (replica, scoring data) or if a bench of vetted judges is built; both are unproven here. [L]

  5. Biggest assumption to test: that a buyer will pay repeatedly for the owner's judgment of media, as a verdict, when nothing in this sweep found anyone selling it (Viral Validator in the synthesis showed $2 all-time). Cheapest test: sell the verdict to 5 agency clients at a fixed price and see whether 3 come back for a second one within 30 days. Rows here do not test it, because they sell writing, a coach replica, and video volume, not a verdict.

  6. Confidence summary: row facts H where quoted from the pages; all arithmetic M; the Brevilabs revenue-source reading L; questions 2, 4 L; question 1 M; question 5 reasoning M, outcome unknown.
