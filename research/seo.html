2. What the table says

  • Money is flowing to measurement: tracking dashboards for "are we cited in ChatGPT / AI Overviews". Funded players cluster there (Profound, Peec, Brandlight, Searchable). Confidence: high.
  • Small independent winners exist but are few: Rank Prompt at $42k MRR is the only verified solo-scale row above $12k. Confidence: medium (list not exhaustive).
  • Many verified rows show near-zero revenue (Viasibility, QuickSEO, SEO GLOBE). The tracker space is crowded and cheap ($29 to $150 entry). Confidence: high.
  • Prices converge at $29 to $150 per month for tracking; agency tiers run $500 to $2,000 (Rank Prompt). Confidence: medium.

3. (1) Where is the gap

Trackers tell a brand its score. They do not tell it what to do, and they do not carry the judgment. Gaps in the buyer's pain:

  1. Score to action. Every tool reports a visibility number; the work of earning citations (which third-party sources the models trust, which claims to publish, which pages to restructure) is left to the buyer. Peec lists "recommended actions" and Profound sells "agents", but price and depth sit at the enterprise end. Confidence: medium (read from vendor pages, not buyer interviews).
  2. People and celebrities. All tools found track company brands and products. I found no tool aimed at how an AI answers "who is X" or "best [category] creator" for a person or talent. Confidence: low (a negative from limited searching).
  3. Agencies need client-ready reads. Rank Prompt's Agency tiers ($1,000 to $2,000) suggest agencies pay; whether they get strategy, not just dashboards, is unknown. Confidence: low.
  4. Trust in the number. Prompt sets and model answers vary run to run; a human who judges which prompts matter for the buyer's market is scarce. Confidence: low (my inference, to test with calls).

4. (2) Which gap fits the owner (research, strategy, AI-native workflow, marketing judgment)

Gap 1 combined with 4: an opinionated "AI-citation strategy" service that uses existing trackers as instruments and sells the diagnosis and the plan. This passes the filter: the human judges peak output (which sources, which claims), AI does the scan and drafting, the moat is the point of view, and it does not compete on a dashboard. Gap 2 (people and talent) is a possible wedge for the celebrity buyer but is low confidence and unproven demand; test it as one offer, not as the lead.

Fail check: a plain tracker would be a wrapper anyone can build (many rows at $0 MRR). Do not build a tracker first.

5. (3) One small paid offer, sellable this month

"AI Answer Audit": fixed price (suggest test at $300 to $500; price is my suggestion, not market data), delivered in 3 days. For one brand or one person: run 30 to 50 real buyer questions across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and Gemini; record who gets named and which sources are cited; deliver a 1 to 2 page memo with the top 5 source gaps and a 30-day action list. Free sample on the prospect's own name to open the conversation. Sell to agencies first (they resell it) and to a few brands or talents in the owner's network. Anchor: Rank Prompt's free first report sells paid plans from $150/mo, so a human-judged audit has a visible ladder. Confidence: medium on demand, low on price.
