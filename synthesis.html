Synthesis, first pass (branch claude-research, 2026-10-30)

Inputs: research/sweep-trustmrr.md (50 rows), sweep-yc.md (57 rows, 811 companies in 2026 batches), sweep-speedrun-markets.md (37 Speedrun rows, 29 market listings), and four marketing-vertical files from a first, too narrow pass. All figures were read by agents from public pages and are not re-checked at source. Funding and revenue on YC and Speedrun profiles are unverified; TrustMRR MRR is Stripe-verified except where the key has expired.

What the three sources agree on

  1. The AI-native firm that sells the outcome is now the main pattern. YC: 30 such companies in 2026 vs 8 in 2025 (law, accounting, ad agency, brokerage, radiology, trucking), 15 in Summer 2026 alone. TrustMRR: AEO Engine (an agency), GojiberryAI and PROSP (agent-run outbound), expert-led services. Medium confidence (keyword counts on one-liners).
  2. Teams keep shrinking. YC teams of 1-2 people: 32% to 50%. Median team 4 to 3.
  3. "Agent" is the baseline, not the edge. 40% of YC companies in both years.
  4. Execution tools are crowded and cheap; the money sits in outcomes. Scheduling, generators, trackers are the crowded rows. Winners with large MRR are outbound agents ($424k, $128k) and search-visibility ($179k top).
  5. YC is moving toward physical industries (Industrials 10% to 19%). The owner has no edge there.

Implication for the thesis: "3 people doing the work of 300" is no longer unusual inside YC. The claim alone will not stand out. What would: a proven vertical with revenue, and an edge competitors cannot copy (here: the owner's judgment of what is peak, plus 3 agencies as the first buyers and data).

Reading

  • Candidate 1 fits the owner and the federation model best, but it has the weakest evidence of demand. Candidate 2 and 3 have verified money but a weaker personal edge.
  • A promising shape is candidate 1 on top of candidate 2 or 3: the verdict and strategy layer sold to buyers who already pay for the tools. This is a hypothesis, not a finding.
  • The marketing vertical files show the same pattern: winners sell tools; nobody found sells an independent verdict. Absence in a US-only search is not proof.

What is missing

  • Demand test for the verdict offer (the cheapest evidence: sell the 48-hour offers to existing clients of the 3 agencies, $150-$500, this month; prices are agent guesses).
  • TrustMRR category pages that 404ed (ads, e-commerce), the 22 unread Speedrun profiles, Southeast Asia, celebrity-specific tools.
  • Outside-marketing themes were scanned, but the owner's edge was judged by the agents from a profile summary, not tested.

Deep dive on candidates 1 and 4 (2026-10-30)

Sources: research/deep-c1.md, research/deep-c4.md. Public pages only; no trials, no logins. Prices below are agent hypotheses unless marked.

Findings

  • Everyone found sells execution, revenue share or monitoring (Adside 10% of spend, Light Anchor 5-15%, Clouted quote-only, Gudea no public price). Nobody found sells a stand-alone human-judged verdict. Either an open slot or no demand; research cannot tell which. (MED)
  • The only paid verdict-before-spend is the old panel pre-testing form, about $3,000-$25,000 per study, from secondary sources. (LOW-MED)
  • Cheap AI scorer data point: Viral Validator earned $1.50 all-time (not $2), listed for sale at $10,000. (HIGH on the number)
  • Expert-judgment businesses that reach $50k+ MRR do it with a defined buyer, a recurring price, a proof story, and a bench or encoded judgment. Windmill Growth: about 30 clients near $4k with a bench of writers; its $117k MRR is unverified (TrustMRR page 404). VirtualJK: 93 subscribers near $520, MRR may be stale. No row shows several experts, so the federation model would be new. (LOW-MED, n=2)
  • Personal brand does not look mandatory; a checkable track record can substitute (10 years calling viral media, 3 agencies, a SaaS exit). (LOW)

Both candidates converge on one assumption: a buyer with real spend pays a stand-alone fee for the owner's verdict, acts on it, and comes back. Only the owner's buyers can answer it.

Test to run before building anything

  • Offer A, pick-the-winner review: 10-20 drafts and a goal in, rank + top 3 with reasons + kill list out in 48 hours.
  • Sell to 10 existing clients of the 3 agencies: first 3 at $99, then $300-500 per batch.
  • Pass: at least 3 paid yeses of 10, and at least 3 buyers return for a second batch within 30 days. Also compare the picks with real results.
  • Fail reading: free interest but no payment means the verdict lives inside the agency relationship, not as a product.

If it passes, shapes B (campaign gate, about $1,500) and the replica trained on the owner's scored calls come next; the replica needs 30-50 owner-scored items first.
